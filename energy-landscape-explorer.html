Energy Landscape Explorer

Pick a symmetric matrix S and see its energy xᵀSx as a surface over the plane: a bowl, a trough or a saddle. The right-hand picture is the same surface seen from straight above, where each curve joins points at the same height, like a contour map. Matches Sections 2, 3 and 5 of the Week 5 Notes.

Matrix S

S = [[a, b], [b, c]]. The off-diagonal entry b appears in both corners, so S is always symmetric.

Tests (Notes, Section 3)

Eigenvalues λ₁, λ₂
Pivots a, det/a
2×2 test: a, det = ac − b²

Probe vector x

Minimize f(x) = ½xᵀSx − bᵀx

Each step is x ← x − η(Sx − b). It starts from the probe; drag the probe to change the start.

What to try

  1. Load C and turn the surface until you can see it is a bowl. Then drag the probe in a full circle in the top view. The energy stays positive in every direction, and the readout shows it as λ₁y₁² + λ₂y₂² with both weights positive.
  2. Load K. Find a direction where the energy is negative. Which eigenvector line is it on, and what is that eigenvalue? Compare the two coloured slices on the surface: at distance t from the origin along qᵢ the height is λᵢt², so one opens up and one opens down.
  3. Load T. Find the line where the energy is zero, and compare it with the null-space direction (2, −1) from the notes.
  4. Start from C and slowly move b from 2 up to 3.5. Watch det = 10 − b² and λ₂ shrink: the bowl gets flatter along q₂, and between b = 3.1 and b = 3.2 det turns negative and the bowl becomes a saddle. (Exactly at b = √10 ≈ 3.16 it would be a trough.)
  5. Switch to Minimize f with C and b = (7, 4). Drag the probe to a corner and run gradient descent with η = 0.2, then η = 0.34. The safe limit 2/λ₁ = 1/3 is shown in the panel.
