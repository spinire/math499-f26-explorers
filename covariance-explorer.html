MATH 499 · Week 4 · Symmetric matrices and the spectral theorem

Covariance Explorer

Each dot is one student: hours studied and quiz score (notes, Table 1). Pick a direction u and every dot casts a shadow on the blue line through the mean. How spread out those shadows are is the variance of the data in the direction u, and it equals uTCu. The guide under the plot explains what that means, why the direction with the largest spread is special, and what eigen-coordinates are good for.

What to try

  1. Point u along the hours axis, then the score axis. Do you get the diagonal entries of C, 5 and 2? Try 45°: the notes say 5.5.
  2. Find the direction with the largest variance without turning on the eigenvectors. What angle and what variance do you get? Now turn them on.
  3. Where is the smallest variance? What is the angle between the two directions?
  4. Switch units to score in percent. Which direction wins now, and why? Then try Standardized (notes §5, cautions).
  5. Drag one point far from the others. How do C, the eigenvectors and the ellipse respond?

Turn the data into eigen-coordinates

Measure every student along q₁ and q₂ instead of along hours and score: z₁ = q₁T(x − mean), z₂ = q₂T(x − mean), or z = QT(x − mean). In the picture the plane turns until q₁ is horizontal, then flips so the vertical axis points along q₂.
