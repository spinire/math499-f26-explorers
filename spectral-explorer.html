MATH 499 · Week 4 · Symmetric matrices and the spectral theorem

Spectral Theorem Explorer

A symmetric n×n matrix S has n mutually perpendicular directions along which it only stretches. The spectral theorem, S = QΛQT, says that is all it does: look at space along those directions, stretch each one by its eigenvalue, and look back. This explorer shows the 2×2 case, where there are two directions and you can see everything in the plane. Drag the blue vector x and follow it to Sx. The guide under the plane walks through the idea one step at a time.

What to try

  1. Covariance matrix: press Play. After step 1 the teal arrow q1 lies on the horizontal axis. In step 2, which direction is stretched by 6 and which is left at length 1?
  2. Stop at step 2. The ellipse has no tilt there. Compare with the quadratic form: 5x₁² + 4x₁x₂ + 2x₂² has a cross term; 6y₁² + y₂² does not (notes §4).
  3. Drag x onto the teal line. Which steps change its direction?
  4. Negative eigenvalue: watch step 2. What happens to the purple arrow as its stretch factor passes through 0?
  5. Repeated eigenvalue: step 1 does nothing useful. Why can any orthonormal pair serve as Q?
